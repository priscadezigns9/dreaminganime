SEASON 1 REVIEW

Tsuredure Children

Ensemble romance works best here as a relay of awkward attempts at communication: each couple has a different way of misreading, hesitating, and trying again. The short-form rhythm lets the show move between relationships before any one pairing becomes the whole story.

Scum’s Wish (Kuzu no Honkai)

The polished image of a couple can conceal two people reaching for someone else. The drama follows loneliness through compromises, unspoken needs, and the decision to pretend that a substitute is enough. Its unease comes from watching those arrangements feel comforting and damaging at once.

Teasing Master Takagi-san

The gentle contest between Takagi and Nishikata is less about who wins a prank than about how they learn to read one another. Repeated teasing gives Nishikata fresh chances to plan a comeback, while small reactions reveal the fondness he cannot quite name. Its relaxed pace makes tiny shifts in confidence the payoff.

Wotakoi: Love Is Hard for Otaku

Shared fandom offers Narumi and Hirotaka an easy opening, but being otaku does not make everyday communication effortless. The workplace setting gives their romance room to grow through routine, friendship, and the ways adults negotiate their private interests in public. Its strongest warmth comes from compatibility being practiced, not presumed.

The Quintessential Quintuplets

Tutoring five sisters is not simply a matter of delivering the same lesson five times. Fuutarou has to earn trust from students with distinct needs and temperaments, making cooperation as important as academic progress. The ensemble premise supplies the comedy, while the gradual change in how each sister responds gives the season its forward motion.

My Teen Romantic Comedy SNAFU

The Service Club sets up a sharp question: is cynicism the same as understanding people? Hachiman’s observations can sound perceptive, yet the season tests what his detached judgments miss about the people around him. The school-club format turns social problem-solving into a debate about motives, pride, and the risks of genuine connection.

The Duke of Death and His Maid

A curse that makes touch dangerous turns ordinary closeness into a negotiation. The Duke and Alice can tease, joke, and share quiet moments, but physical distance remains a constant reminder of what they cannot yet have. That literal barrier gives their flirtation a bittersweet edge without removing its playful energy.

Horimiya

Hori and Miyamura become a couple early, so the season’s interest shifts toward what intimacy changes. Their private selves complicate the roles classmates assign them, and knowing each other more closely alters how they see the wider group. The lightness of the school comedy works best when it makes room for those changing social perceptions.

The Angel Next Door Spoils Me Rotten

Meals and shared routines become the quiet language through which Mahiru and Amane grow more comfortable around one another. The season builds its romance from small acts of consideration rather than grand declarations, giving everyday care the weight of a meaningful choice. Its appeal lies in the gradual shift from convenience to trust.

My Love Story with Yamada-kun at Lv999

Akane’s expressive, social style and Yamada’s reserve make their contrast immediately legible, but neither character fits neatly into one mode. The online game is a meeting point, not a substitute for offline connection. The season finds comedy in that gap and warmth in watching each learn how the other communicates.
