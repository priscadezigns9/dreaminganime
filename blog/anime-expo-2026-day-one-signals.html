Anime Expo 2026: What the Official Program Confirmed

← BACK TO THE LOG

Anime Expo 2026 ran July 2–5 in Los Angeles. This recap uses the event organizer’s published program and post-event release. It is not an eyewitness report, and it separates events that were scheduled at the convention from announcements made before it.

What the organizers reported

In its July 10 wrap-up, Anime Expo’s organizer, the Society for the Promotion of Japanese Animation, reported turnstile attendance of more than 422,000 fans from over 65 countries and more than 1,400 hours of programming. “Turnstile attendance” is the organizer’s measure; it should not be read as a count of unique people unless the organizer defines it that way.

The organizer said programming took place across an expanded downtown Los Angeles footprint, including the Los Angeles Convention Center, Crypto.com Arena, The Novo and Peacock Theater. Its recap also described premieres, screenings, panels, concerts, cosplay showcases and special guests.

Premieres listed on the official schedule

The pre-event schedule identified the Kagurabachi anime as a world premiere at Anime Expo. The adaptation itself had already been announced on April 27, with an April 2027 premiere date. Anime Expo’s program listed a premiere event; it did not originate the adaptation announcement.

The schedule also listed the Episode 1 premiere of BLACK TORCH, a Season 2 premiere for Black Clover, and an exclusive first look at Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2. These are program highlights, not claims that every title was announced for the first time at the convention.

Separating advance news from convention coverage

Several details in the old version of this article were described as if they had been observed on site or announced during Day One, but the post did not provide evidence for that framing. This revision removes those statements, along with unsupported crowd descriptions, merchandising claims and the Hunter × Hunter volume “street date” claim.

The useful takeaway is narrower and verifiable: Anime Expo’s published schedule offered a broad mix of premieres and industry programming, and the organizer later reported the event’s attendance and programming totals.
