KAGUYA-SAMA: LOVE IS WAR SEASON 1 / SERIES REVIEW

Kaguya-sama: Love Is War Season 1

Two brilliant student-council leaders turn mutual affection into a battle of elaborate schemes because neither wants to confess first.

← BACK TO REVIEWS
SPOILER-LIGHT REVIEW · Season 1, episodes 1–12 only. Later seasons, OVAs, and specials are not included.
QUICK TAKE

Season 1 of Kaguya-sama: Love Is War turns a simple crush into a string of escalating psychological-comedy set pieces. Kaguya and Shirogane’s pride fuels the premise, while Chika, Ishigami, and the student council keep the show from relying on one joke. Its sharp timing and expressive inner monologues make the romantic stalemate consistently funny, with some suggestive humour.

CONFESS FIRST, LOSE

Kaguya Shinomiya and Miyuki Shirogane are the student council’s most capable members, and each is convinced the other should confess first. The series frames romance as a contest in which a successful scheme can become a humiliating defeat if the opponent notices what it means.

That premise gives the show a reliable comic engine without requiring the leads to be cruel. Their pride, insecurity, and earnest feelings all feed the overthinking, and the result is less a conventional love triangle than a series of miniature strategic disasters.

THE COUNCIL’S COMIC RHYTHM

The narrator’s dramatic framing turns small school interactions into high-stakes operations. Visual exaggeration, inner monologues, and deliberate pauses make the smallest misunderstanding feel like a tactical crisis. The best episodes keep the schemes brief enough for the character reactions to deliver the punchline.

Chika Fujiwara’s unpredictability, Kaguya’s social blind spots, and Shirogane’s carefully maintained competence give the ensemble distinct comic roles. Ishigami adds a drier counterpoint. The show broadens its humour through them rather than simply repeating the confession challenge.

ROMANCE WITH A SHARP EDGE

The first 12 episodes balance episodic comedy with small changes in how the leads understand one another. The emotional movement is incremental; this season’s pleasure is watching them get close enough to reveal vulnerability and then immediately try to regain the upper hand.

Crunchyroll’s content advisory says not recommended under 14 and flags suggestive dialogue, profanity, sexualized imagery, and smoking. Viewers who want gentle, low-key romance may find the comedy too performative, but the season is a strong fit for fans of clever ensemble rom-coms.

VERDICT

Kaguya-sama: Love Is War Season 1 makes romantic hesitation into a witty, highly stylized contest, then gives its characters enough warmth to make the rivalry matter. The comedy is energetic and occasionally suggestive; this review stops after episode 12.
