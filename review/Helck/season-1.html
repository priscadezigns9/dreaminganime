HELCK: SEASON 1

A TOURNAMENT WITH A QUESTION INSIDE IT

Three months after a human hero defeats the Demon Lord, demons hold a tournament to choose a successor. Helck, a powerful human who says he hates humanity, enters. Vermilio, one of the realm’s rulers, distrusts him and tries to uncover his motive. That suspicion gives the tournament its comic tension and the story its central mystery.

The comedy comes from the gap between Helck’s cheerful manner and Vermilio’s certainty that something is wrong. Her schemes keep the tournament moving while his unexplained allegiance raises the stakes.

COMEDY THAT STARTS TO CARRY WEIGHT

Anime News Network’s Episodes 1–2 review notes that the anime introduces dramatic hints earlier than the manga. That can make the later shift feel less abrupt, but it also makes Helck’s hidden motives less uncertain and some early jokes less effective. The anime trades surprise for foreshadowing.

Otaku USA likewise describes a comic opening that gives way to a more serious fantasy conflict. Viewers who enjoy that kind of tonal change may find the mix appealing.

THE PACE IS THE MAIN TRADE-OFF

Crunchyroll announced a 24-episode, two-cour run. Anime News Network notes that the first two episodes cover much of the first manga volume. The brisk adaptation reaches the larger conflict quickly, but gives some early comedy and character moments less room to land.

The Episode 24 season review notes that the anime ends with more manga story left to adapt. Treat this as an unfinished chapter, not a complete adaptation.

WHO MAY ENJOY IT

Try Helck if you enjoy fantasy comedy that grows more serious and don’t mind a quick adaptation or an unresolved ending. If you want consistent light comedy or a self-contained story, this may be a frustrating fit.

SEASON 1 VERDICT

The tournament is a lively setup for a story about trust and divided loyalties. Vermilio’s suspicion plays well against Helck’s easy confidence, and the growing mystery gives their journey direction. The anime moves quickly and ends before the manga’s story is complete. It is best approached as a fantasy comedy that darkens over time, not as a self-contained or consistently light series.
