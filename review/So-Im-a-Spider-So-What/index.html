SO I’M A SPIDER, SO WHAT?

A dungeon crawl where clever survival, comic persistence, and a wider mystery keep the story moving.

SERIES REVIEW · ISEKAI / FANTASY / SURVIVAL
QUICK TAKE

So I’m a Spider, So What? gives its heroine one of the genre’s most unusual starting points: a high school girl wakes up as a tiny spider in a dungeon full of things trying to eat her. The show’s funniest, strongest engine is her relentless inner monologue as she learns to survive. Its split storytelling and uneven CG animation can make the bigger picture less smooth.

A SPIDER’S-EYE VIEW OF ISEKAI

The premise is immediate and playful: a high school girl is reborn as a spider monster in a deadly dungeon. She starts at the bottom of the food chain, with no friendly party and no guarantee that the next room will be survivable. Her weapon is quick thinking, stubborn optimism, and the willingness to treat every narrow escape as a lesson.

That makes the dungeon chapters feel refreshingly physical. The protagonist has to read the environment, improvise with limited abilities, and make small victories count. Her running commentary gives the show a comic pulse, turning skill upgrades and monster encounters into a one-girl campaign against increasingly ridiculous odds.

THE MYSTERY BEHIND THE DUNGEON

The anime gradually widens its focus beyond the spider’s survival. A second storyline follows human characters in the same fantasy world, and the contrast between those scenes and the dungeon crawl becomes part of the puzzle. The early separation can feel abrupt, but the structure rewards viewers who enjoy piecing together how the two tracks connect.

The balance is best when the show lets the viewer compare its very different kinds of survival. The spider’s progress is immediate and game-like; the human thread brings politics, relationships, and a different scale of danger. Together, they hint at a world with more history and consequence than the first dungeon floor suggests.

WHERE THE ADAPTATION WOBBLES

The central performance and comic timing are strong, but the production is not equally consistent across every scene. Some CG action and creature shots look noticeably different from the more conventional character animation, and the switch between storylines can interrupt the momentum of a particularly good survival sequence.

The finale also leaves major questions open, so viewers looking for a fully self-contained story may come away wanting a clearer resolution. That cliff edge is less frustrating if the journey itself is the attraction: watching a weak monster become more resourceful is the show’s most satisfying through-line.

VERDICT

So I’m a Spider, So What? is an entertaining pick for fans of isekai survival, dungeon crawls, and protagonists who can turn panic into a plan. Kumoko’s humor and adaptability make the series distinctive, while the second storyline adds a worthwhile mystery. The uneven visuals and abrupt structural shifts are noticeable, but they do not erase the fun of its central survival adventure.
