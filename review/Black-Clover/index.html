BLACK CLOVER / SERIES REVIEW

BLACK CLOVER

An underdog’s anti-magic, a fierce rivalry, and a long journey toward Wizard King.

← BACK TO REVIEWS
EDITORIAL SERIES REVIEW · ACTION / FANTASY
QUICK TAKE

Black Clover is a familiar battle-shonen underdog story with a strong central contrast: Asta is born without magic in a world built around it, while his gifted rival Yuno shares the same dream of becoming Wizard King. Anti-magic gives Asta a distinctive way to fight, and the Black Bulls give the long adventure its rowdy heart. The tradeoff is pacing: the 170-episode original run sometimes leans on loud humor and familiar escalation beats.

A RIVALRY BUILT ON A SHARED PROMISE

Black Clover opens with a clean shonen engine. Asta and Yuno are orphans raised together, both aiming for the title of Wizard King. Yuno has exceptional magic; Asta has none at all. The gap between them makes their rivalry more than a scoreboard: they push each other forward while carrying very different ideas of what effort, talent, and recognition mean.

Asta’s refusal to give up is the series’ loudest trait, sometimes literally. The early comedy can make him a lot to take in, but his determination has a clear story purpose: he is not waiting for the world to grant him the tools it values. He has to find another way to compete.

ANTI-MAGIC MAKES THE UNDERDOG HOOK WORK

In a world where magic shapes status and combat, Asta’s anti-magic is a satisfying counterpoint. It changes the rules of a fight instead of simply making him a weaker version of the gifted characters around him. That gives the series a reliable source of tactical reversals and lets the underdog premise grow into its own kind of power fantasy.

The scope widens as Asta joins the Black Bulls. Their messy, mismatched energy gives the story a second centre beyond the Asta–Yuno rivalry. Team missions, clashing personalities, and gradual trust help the world feel larger than a sequence of duels. The series is at its best when a battle tests the group’s connections as much as its magic.

THE LONG-RUN SHONEN TRADEOFF

The original TV run spans 170 episodes, which gives the cast time to grow but also asks for patience. Some stretches lean on repeated training, rescue, and power-escalation rhythms. Asta’s relentless volume is part of the character, but the early episodes can make the journey feel more familiar than the premise deserves.

That uneven start is balanced by a strong ensemble and a clear destination. Yuno remains a meaningful rival rather than a one-note obstacle, and the Black Bulls’ oddball team dynamic adds warmth to the conflict. The series does not reinvent battle shonen, but it understands why determination, rivalry, and found family keep viewers invested.

VERDICT

Black Clover is an easy recommendation for fans of long-form action fantasy who like visible character growth, team battles, and a protagonist who has to fight the rules of his world. It is less suited to viewers who want a quick, tightly paced story or find loud comic leads exhausting. The original 170-episode run concluded in 2021; Crunchyroll announced a Season 2 broadcast for October 2026 and said it would continue streaming the series.
