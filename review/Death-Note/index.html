DEATH NOTE / SERIES REVIEW

Death Note

A supernatural crime thriller about a notebook that turns the power to judge life and death into an escalating contest of intellect, secrecy, and moral certainty.

← BACK TO REVIEWS
SPOILER-LIGHT REVIEW · The 2006 television anime, complete at 37 episodes. This review does not cover recap specials or live-action adaptations.
QUICK TAKE

Death Note builds a gripping cat-and-mouse thriller around Light Yagami, the lethal notebook, and detective L. Its strongest hook is the tactical duel and the way power tests the user’s idea of justice. The escalating mind games are stylish and compelling, though the later turns can feel more heightened than the chilling early premise.

A NOTEBOOK, A NEW ORDER

When high-school student Light Yagami discovers a notebook that can kill anyone whose name is written in it, he sees an opportunity to remake society. The supernatural rules give the story a clean, unnerving premise: a private decision can become an invisible sentence, while the person wielding that power remains beyond ordinary suspicion.

The arrival of the shinigami Ryuk adds dark humor and distance without softening the consequences. Light’s project quickly attracts the attention of L, an unconventional detective whose investigation gives the series its central duel.

THE GAME OF INFORMATION

The most engaging stretches turn small clues, surveillance, and carefully staged conversations into action. Light and L are memorable not because the show asks viewers to accept either as a simple hero, but because each treats intelligence as a weapon and pushes the other to reveal just enough.

The internal logic of the notebook keeps the cat-and-mouse game legible while the number of players and tactics grows. At times, the plotting becomes so elaborate that suspense gives way to dramatic one-upmanship. That heightened style is part of the show’s identity, even when it strains credibility.

JUSTICE UNDER PRESSURE

The series’ moral tension comes from the distance between claiming to punish wrongdoing and assuming the right to decide who deserves to live. Light’s conviction and the public response around Kira make the story a dark study of authority, fear, and the appetite for simple answers.

Death Note remains a sharply paced supernatural thriller for viewers who enjoy strategy, ethical ambiguity, and an increasingly intense contest of wills. Its violence and treatment of suicide are important considerations; the official VIZ and Crunchyroll pages offer context, but current streaming playback varies by territory.

VERDICT

Death Note makes a supernatural object the starting point for a memorable battle over power and justice. The first season’s mystery, rival detectives, and escalating strategies are its enduring strengths; viewers should be ready for a dark tone and violence.

Content note: Not recommended for viewers under 14. The official advisory lists violence, suicide, and smoking.
