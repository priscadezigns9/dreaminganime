DARLING IN THE FRANXX / SERIES REVIEW

DARLING IN THE FRANXX

A mecha romance about identity, connection, and the price of being useful.

← BACK TO REVIEWS
EDITORIAL SERIES REVIEW
QUICK TAKE

Darling in the Franxx pairs an arresting dystopian-mecha setting with a memorable Hiro and Zero Two relationship, but its late-story turns and rushed ending make the full journey uneven.

Dreaming Anime is a Trinidad-rooted, Caribbean anime publication for fans worldwide.

A ROMANCE BUILT INSIDE A WAR

In a desolate future, children are kept apart from the outside world in Mistilteinn, the “birdcage,” where their purpose is to pilot giant FranXX. Hiro once looked destined for that role; after he falls behind, the system treats him as unnecessary. Meeting Zero Two disrupts that verdict and gives the story its central tension: are these young pilots people with futures of their own, or instruments built for someone else’s survival?

The premise gives the mecha action an emotional foundation. The machines and battles are striking, but the stronger question is what it costs to grow up inside a system that measures children by their usefulness. Hiro’s relationship with Zero Two brings that question close to the surface, mixing attraction, loneliness, fear, and the desire to be accepted without reducing their bond to a simple rescue fantasy.

HIRO AND ZERO TWO ARE THE ANCHOR

Zero Two has a vivid presence from the start: confident, playful, and carrying a history that makes closeness difficult. Hiro’s uncertainty makes him an effective counterweight. Their connection can be messy and melodramatic, but it gives the series a clear emotional centre and makes its best quieter scenes land. The pair’s chemistry is also what keeps the wider worldbuilding from feeling like machinery without people inside it.

The surrounding squad adds warmth and useful contrast, especially when the story lets the pilots act like young people rather than symbols in a conflict. Those moments make the stakes feel personal. The trade-off is that the ensemble’s development is uneven: some relationships receive memorable attention, while others can feel like they are waiting for the main plot to make room for them.

WHERE THE STORY CHANGES GEAR

The early stretch is strongest when mystery, romantic tension, and the squad’s daily life build together. Later, the story expands its ideas and raises the scale of the conflict. That ambition is compelling in places, but the shift can feel abrupt: intimate questions about identity and belonging are asked to carry increasingly large revelations before every character has had time to process them. The final movement is emotionally sincere, yet its speed and choices remain divisive.

That unevenness is why the series can be both easy to remember and hard to recommend without a caveat. Its visual identity, music, and central relationship give it real force; its pacing and late-series turns do not always support those strengths with equal care.

VERDICT

Darling in the Franxx is a striking, emotionally charged mecha romance whose best moments come from Hiro and Zero Two trying to find a real connection in a world that assigns them a purpose. It is worth watching if you enjoy character-focused science fiction and can tolerate sharp tonal shifts. Go in expecting an uneven final stretch rather than a universally satisfying payoff.

Content advisory: Crunchyroll lists violence, suggestive dialogue, and sexualized imagery, and does not recommend the series for viewers under 14.

QUICK ANSWERS

What is Darling in the Franxx about?

In a desolate future, children are isolated in Mistilteinn and expected to pilot giant mechs called FranXX. Hiro’s meeting with Zero Two changes his place in that system and the direction of his life.

Is Darling in the Franxx worth watching?

The series is worth considering for its Hiro and Zero Two relationship, visual style, and questions about identity and connection. Its later tonal shifts and rushed, divisive ending may not work for every viewer.
