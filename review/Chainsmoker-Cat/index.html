CHAINSMOKER CAT / SERIES REVIEW

CHAINSMOKER CAT

A chaotic catgirl comedy about cigarettes, rent, work, and the friends who keep trying.

← BACK TO REVIEWS
EDITORIAL SERIES REVIEW · COMEDY
QUICK TAKE

Chainsmoker Cat turns Yani’s smoking habit, rent trouble, and unstable work life into rough, absurdist comedy. Its best angle is the tension between her self-destructive routines and the friends who keep showing up; its main risk is leaning on the same shock gag until the characters stop moving forward.

A MESSY LIFE, NOT A CUTE CAT FANTASY

Yani—Yanineko in the cast listing—is a catgirl whose days are shaped by a serious smoking habit, unpaid rent, and jobs she struggles to keep. The people around her worry, offer help, and keep getting pulled into the consequences. That combination gives the series a clear comic engine: ordinary pressures keep colliding with Yani’s refusal, or inability, to get herself together.

The catgirl premise is deliberately rough around the edges. Chainsmoker Cat is not a gentle story about a pet-like mascot; Netflix rates it TV-MA, and smoking is central to the comedy. The show’s most useful contrast is between Yani’s chaotic self-image and the fact that rent, work, and other people’s patience do not disappear just because she wants to avoid them.

THE PEOPLE AROUND YANI GIVE IT HEART

There is more to work with here than a cigarette gag. Yani’s sister and friends are the pressure points that make the premise human: they care, they get frustrated, and they have to decide how much support is actually helping. That uneasy mix of affection and exasperation is where the series can turn a loud comedy into a character story.

When the humor comes from that push and pull, Yani’s flaws have consequences and the people around her feel like people rather than a chorus of reactions. It also gives the story a grounded subject underneath the absurdity: trying to change a harmful habit is harder than promising to quit once.

WHEN THE SHOCK JOKE BECOMES THE WHOLE JOKE

The same premise creates the main weakness. Smoking, mess, and bad decisions are easy to escalate for a quick laugh, but repetition can make Yani feel frozen in place. The story is strongest when a gag changes a relationship or makes daily life harder; if the scene only tops the last gross-out moment, the surprise wears off quickly.

That is the line this adaptation has to walk: keep its unruly comic voice without treating nicotine dependence as a cute accessory. The friends’ concern and Yani’s practical problems offer a way to do that, because they give the comedy consequences instead of asking the audience to laugh at the habit alone.

VERDICT

Chainsmoker Cat is a deliberately scruffy adult comedy for viewers who enjoy chaotic leads and irreverent slice-of-life humor. Its premise is memorable, and the support network around Yani gives it room for more than shock comedy. But the smoking-centered gags can become repetitive, and viewers who want a comforting cat anime should look elsewhere. Netflix lists the 2026 series at TV-MA and shows 12 episodes; Crunchyroll reports that episode 12 concluded the run.

Content note: Smoking and nicotine dependence are central themes, alongside financial stress and adult comedy. Netflix rates the series TV-MA; check the current local listing if you need more detailed advisories.
Adaptation notes: Nyan Nyan Factory’s manga is serialized in Kodansha’s Weekly Young Magazine; Crunchyroll reported 13 volumes published in Japan and an English edition from Seven Seas Entertainment. The anime is directed by Taku Kimura at Bilbury Animation Studios, with scripts by Takashi Aoshima, character designs by Riki Matsuura, and music by Keichi Suzuki.
