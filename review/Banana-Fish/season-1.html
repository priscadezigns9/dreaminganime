ANIME SERIES REVIEW · SEASON 1

Banana Fish: Season 1

A New York crime mystery puts loyalty and trust under pressure.

← ALL ANIME REVIEWS
Review scope: Banana Fish television series, Season 1 · Episodes 1–24. This review covers the complete 24-episode TV run only; it does not review the manga or other adaptations.
SPOILER-LIGHT · SEASON 1 · 24 EPISODES
QUICK TAKE

Banana Fish uses a crime-thriller frame to examine how trust can survive in a world where nearly every relationship is exposed to leverage. Ash and Eiji’s connection gives the conspiracy a human center, while the series’ mature subject matter makes its warnings essential reading.

LOYALTY IS A RISK

The bond between Ash and Eiji matters because they enter the story with radically different kinds of power and vulnerability. Their growing trust is not a convenient escape from the danger around them; it is something both must protect while other people try to control the terms of their lives.

A THRILLER WITH PERSONAL CONSEQUENCES

The mystery of “Banana Fish” gives the season momentum, but the crime plot is strongest when it changes what safety, freedom, and friendship can mean for the people caught in it. The New York setting keeps the stakes close to the characters rather than turning the conspiracy into an abstract puzzle.

WHO THIS SEASON SUITS

Viewers looking for a tense, character-led crime drama may find the mix of suspense and emotional commitment compelling. The series also deals with exploitation, violence, and substance-related harm; its intensity is a central part of the experience, not incidental background.

VERDICT

Banana Fish is a high-stakes crime story whose most lasting subject is the fragile work of choosing whom to trust. Its 24-episode structure lets the conspiracy and the Ash–Eiji relationship develop together. The mature content advisory should be considered before starting.

Content note: TV-14; violence, substance use, alcohol use, smoking, foul language, and sexual content. Ratings and availability may vary by region.
