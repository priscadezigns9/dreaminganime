HELL GIRL / ORIGINAL SERIES REVIEW

Hell Girl

A midnight grievance can summon Ai Enma, but revenge comes with a price that outlasts the target. The episodic engine is strong; its repetition is the risk.

← BACK TO REVIEWS
THE MIDNIGHT CORRESPONDENCE
This review covers the original 2005–06 Hell Girl television series (Jigoku Shōjo), not its later seasons or adaptations. Source-informed editorial review; not a firsthand watch report.
QUICK TAKE

The first Hell Girl series builds a memorable supernatural bargain: ask Ai Enma to send someone to Hell, and the requester accepts a consequence of their own. Its stand-alone grievances make the moral question immediate, but the repeated cycle of torment, retaliation, and price can grow predictable before the larger character story gathers force.

Dreaming Anime is a Trinidad-rooted, Caribbean anime publication for fans worldwide.

A BARGAIN THAT DOES NOT END AT MIDNIGHT

Jigoku Tsūshin is an urban legend with a practical appeal: at midnight, someone consumed by a grudge can name the person they want punished. Ai Enma carries out that request, but the story makes clear that the decision does not end with the target. The petitioner also binds themselves to a cost after death.

That rule gives the anthology a better question than “does this person deserve revenge?” It asks what desperation can make a person choose, and whether a punishment can settle anything once the harm has spread. The strongest cases leave room for anger and sympathy without pretending that the two cancel each other out.

THE POWER AND LIMIT OF THE EPISODIC FORM

The recurring ritual creates a sharp, recognizable structure. A grievance grows; the person seeking help confronts the bargain; Ai and her attendants carry out its terms. That consistency makes the moral stakes legible, while the distinct victims and conflicts keep many individual episodes from feeling interchangeable.

But an anthology built on one mechanism also risks becoming mechanical. When an episode moves through the same stages without adding a new complication or changing how the characters understand the service, the rhythm starts to feel familiar rather than chilling. The ongoing thread around Ai and those who investigate the website matters because it gives the series somewhere to go beyond the next case.

VERDICT

The original Hell Girl is most compelling when the supernatural contract sharpens an ugly human choice instead of merely delivering a revenge fantasy. Its central rule is memorable, its tone is controlled, and its best stories resist easy moral closure. Expect significant dark material and some repetition across the 26-episode season.

The original series is listed at 26 episodes. Anime News Network classifies its objectionable content as significant. It deals with revenge, bullying, death, and damnation.
