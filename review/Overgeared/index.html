ANIME SERIES REVIEW · EARLY SEASON

Overgeared

An unlucky gamer becomes Grid, then inherits a legendary blacksmith class after a quest resets his stats.

← BACK TO REVIEWS
SPOILER-LIGHT · SEASON 1 · EARLY REVIEW (EPISODES 1–4 LISTED)
QUICK TAKE

Overgeared gives its VR-game power fantasy a useful twist: Grid’s fresh start comes through blacksmithing, not just bigger numbers. The first four episodes establish a fun crafting-and-progression loop, while leaving the most important question open: will Grid grow as a person as quickly as his gear improves?

A QUEST THAT RESETS THE SCORE

Shin Youngwoo logs into Satisfy, a VR game where players can turn in-game achievements into real-world fame and fortune. As Grid, he is an unlucky, low-level player in both worlds. A difficult quest changes his path by making him heir to the knowledge of a legendary blacksmith—but the reward resets his stats to zero.

THE BEST HOOK IS THE CRAFTING

That reset could have been a simple level-up gimmick. Instead, the blacksmith class gives Grid a concrete way to rebuild: learn the craft, make better equipment, and use those creations to change what he can do next. The gear loop gives the familiar game-world progression a more tactile identity than a succession of stat screens.

The show’s strongest promise is in treating creation as part of the adventure. When an item has to be made before it can change the next encounter, the work itself can carry stakes—not just the final power spike.

GRID STILL HAS TO GROW INTO HIS POWER

Grid starts out frustrated and mediocre rather than instantly heroic. That is useful material for a character arc, but the equipment upgrades need to be matched by real personal growth. If luck and increasingly powerful items do all the work, the emotional side will feel thinner than the game mechanics.

FOUR EPISODES INTO THE GAME

Crunchyroll currently lists four Season 1 episodes, from “Legendary Class” and “First Step Towards Being a Blacksmith” to “Item Crafting Face-Off.” The early run introduces the class change and makes crafting central to Grid’s progress; it is not enough to judge the season’s eventual payoff.

VERDICT

Overgeared has a lively hook for fans of gaming fantasy, crafting, and visible progression. Its blacksmith angle gives the power fantasy a distinct engine, and Grid’s reset sets up a satisfying climb from square one. The series is still early, though: the best version of this story will make his character development as rewarding as the gear he builds.
