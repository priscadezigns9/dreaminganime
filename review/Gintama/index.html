GINTAMA

Main TV anime review · spoiler-light · 367 episodes
QUICK TAKE

Gintama can jump from a ridiculous odd-job gag to a sincere character drama without losing its core voice. Gintoki and the Yorozuya make an alien-occupied Edo feel lived in, and the long run gives its best arcs room to pay off. The scale is also a commitment: hundreds of episodes and comedy built around references reward viewers who settle in.

ODD JOBS IN AN ALIEN-OCCUPIED EDO

After aliens reshape Edo, former samurai Gintoki Sakata earns a living taking almost any job alongside Shinpachi and Kagura. Crunchyroll’s official description captures the simple premise; the main TV run spans 367 episodes from 2006 to 2018. This review is about that TV anime, not the later movies or spin-offs.

COMEDY THAT CAN TURN ON A DIME

The series’ range is its signature. Running jokes, parody, and everyday disasters build a familiar rhythm, then longer arcs make the characters’ loyalties and losses matter. The variety keeps the show from feeling like one extended gag, though the humour can be referential and its sheer length asks for patience. Not every episode is meant to advance a single plot.

VERDICT

Gintama is a singular long-form comedy with real emotional depth and a flexible action side. It is best approached as a world to spend time in, not a tightly serialized binge. The linked Blu-ray is the concluding movie, not a release of the full TV run.

Crunchyroll marks the series as not recommended for minors under 14 and lists suggestive dialogue, sexualized imagery, violence, and profanity.
