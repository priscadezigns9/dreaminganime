ASSASSINATION CLASSROOM / SERIES REVIEW

Assassination Classroom

A class of misfits is tasked with killing the teacher who may destroy Earth. What begins as an absurd school-action premise becomes a warm, surprisingly thoughtful story about learning, confidence, and the people who see potential in you.

← BACK TO REVIEWS
SPOILER-LIGHT REVIEW · The original 47-episode television anime: Season 1 (22 episodes) and Season 2 (25 episodes). This review does not cover the later compilation or spin-off films.
QUICK TAKE

Assassination Classroom turns a bizarre mission into an affectionate school story. Class 3-E must try to kill Koro-sensei before he destroys Earth, but the real heart is watching a teacher help students who have been written off discover what they can do. Its comedy, inventive classroom action, and emotional payoff work best when the story lets its ensemble grow together.

Dreaming Anime is a Trinidad-rooted, Caribbean anime publication for fans worldwide.

A CLASS GIVEN UP ON

Kunugigaoka Junior High separates its lowest-ranked students into Class 3-E, up the mountain and away from the main campus. Their new teacher is Koro-sensei: a fast, tentacled being with extraordinary powers, a stated plan to destroy the Earth, and an offer that turns every lesson into a strange assignment. The students are expected to assassinate him, with a huge reward hanging over the attempt.

That premise could have been little more than a string of assassination gags. Instead, the series uses the classroom setup to ask what education looks like when students are treated as failures. Koro-sensei is a target, but he is also the first adult who consistently notices what each pupil needs and helps them improve on their own terms.

LESSONS, LAUGHS, AND CLASSROOM ACTION

The comedy comes from the contrast between Koro-sensei’s cheerful teaching and the absurd danger of the mission. The assassination attempts give the show playful action set-pieces, while the lessons and class dynamics keep the story grounded in the students’ everyday lives. The wide ensemble means some characters receive more space than others, and the early episodic rhythm can make the progression feel gradual.

Nagisa’s quiet observation and Karma’s confidence create useful points of contrast, but the broader class is part of the appeal. Students who first seem defined by a label or a skill gradually become people with different strengths, fears, and ambitions. The show’s best recurring idea is not that everyone becomes a prodigy; it is that a fair chance and attentive guidance can help people see their own possibilities.

WHEN THE JOKE STARTS TO HURT

Underneath the bright color and comic timing is a deadline that gives the story its emotional weight. The central question is not simply whether the students can reach their target. It is what it means to learn from someone when the relationship has an impossible ending built into it. That tension makes the series’ later turns land more deeply than its goofy premise first suggests.

The balance is not perfect: some jokes and recurring character traits can be broad, and the early chapters of the story may feel formulaic to viewers waiting for the larger plot to move. But as the students’ relationships with their teacher deepen, the classroom routines gain meaning. The show earns its sentiment by building that bond across the whole class.

VERDICT

Assassination Classroom is a funny, heartfelt ensemble story wrapped in a high-concept action-comedy. Its premise is memorable, but its strongest achievement is making the students’ growth feel connected to the strange teacher at the center of their mission. Recommended for viewers who enjoy school comedies with a strong cast, energetic action, and an emotional finish. Expect cartoon violence, assassination themes, and some sexualized humor.
