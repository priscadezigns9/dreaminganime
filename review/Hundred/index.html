Official series key art via Crunchyroll.

SERIES REVIEW · SCI-FI ACTION · SCHOOL BATTLE · SPOILER-LIGHT · 12 EPISODES · UPDATED NOVEMBER 27, 2026
QUICK TAKE

Hundred adds an alien threat and transformable combat technology to the familiar gifted-student-at-a-military-academy setup. Its battleship school and mysterious roommate give the premise a couple of hooks, but its early story leans heavily on well-worn academy-duel and harem beats. It suits viewers who enjoy that formula; those looking for a fresh character drama may find the familiar pieces do most of the work.

REVIEW SCOPE: source-informed and spoiler-light, based on the series synopsis, official production information, episode listings and published criticism of the first episode. The 12-episode 2016 TV anime is complete. This review does not claim a firsthand viewing.

AN ACADEMY BUILT FOR A WAR

In Hundred, mysterious creatures called Savages threaten Earth, and the Hundred is the weapon technology that can stand against them. Hayato Kisaragi enters Little Garden, a battleship that doubles as a school and Slayer-training base. Before he can settle in, he is pulled toward a duel with Claire Harvey, the academy’s formidable “Queen.”

The setting gives the school-battle premise a concrete purpose: training is connected to an outside threat, not just a ladder of student rankings. The Hundred’s different forms also offer room for visual action and character-specific powers. It is an easy setup to understand, even if the series’ opening quickly relies on genre shorthand.

FAMILIAR BEATS, FRONT AND CENTRE

Hayato arrives with exceptional compatibility and immediately finds himself at the centre of the school’s attention. A rivalry with Claire, a roommate with a personal connection, and classmates who orbit the protagonist put the show squarely in the academy-action and harem lane.

That is both its selling point and its main limitation. Viewers who like predictable character roles, quick romantic tension and repeated school challenges may find the formula comfortable. Anime News Network’s first-episode preview criticism also called out the familiar setup and heavy explanatory dialogue; that is a useful warning for anyone hoping the series will reinvent its subgenre. It is criticism of the opening episode, not a claim that every later episode does exactly the same thing.

THE ROOMMATE MYSTERY ADDS A PERSONAL THREAD

Emil Crossford, Hayato’s roommate, seems to recognize him in a way Hayato cannot explain. That question gives the opening more than an academy ranking to follow and adds a small thread of personal mystery to the larger Savage conflict.

The available story framing keeps this mystery alongside duels, school relationships and the external threat rather than presenting Hundred as a tightly focused mystery. Its appeal is therefore strongest when a viewer wants several familiar ingredients at once, not one unusually deep worldbuilding or character thread.

PRODUCTION, THEMES AND CONTENT

The television anime was directed by Tomoki Kobayashi at Production IMS, with series composition by Hideki Shirane and character design by Norie Tanaka. The opening theme, “BLOODRED,” is performed by D-selections. The series uses the contrast between school life and large-scale monster combat as its central visual and tonal engine.

Crunchyroll’s current series listing identifies the show as action, romance and science fiction, and flags sexual content, nudity, violence and smoking. The fan-service is part of the show’s presentation, not an incidental detail, so viewers who prefer less sexualized comedy should take that into account.

VERDICT

Hundred is a straightforward pick for viewers who want a complete, 12-episode school-battle sci-fi series with alien enemies, weapon transformations, rivalries and harem comedy. Its setting gives the familiar setup a clear action framework, but the opening does not establish a particularly novel take on the academy formula.

Try it if familiar light-novel action tropes are part of the appeal. If you want distinctive character writing, restrained fan-service or a more unusual science-fiction premise, there are stronger reasons to choose another show first.

CONTENT NOTE: alien combat and peril, violence, sexualized fan-service, nudity and smoking; the review avoids plot spoilers.
