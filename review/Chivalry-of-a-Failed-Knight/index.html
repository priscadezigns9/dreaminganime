QUICK TAKE

Chivalry of a Failed Knight starts with a familiar academy duel, then gives its under-ranked lead a clear reason to fight beyond proving everyone wrong. Ikki and Stella’s rivalry becomes an unusually direct romantic partnership, adding warmth to the tournament structure. Its action and relationship develop briskly, though the series includes sexualized imagery and an early compromising scene.

THE “WORST ONE” GETS A REAL TEST

At Hagun Academy, Blazers manifest their souls as weapons. Ikki Kurogane is rated F because his magical power is low, while Stella Vermillion is an A-ranked prodigy and princess. Their first encounter leads to a duel with serious stakes, setting up Ikki’s effort to prove what discipline and skill can do when raw magical output is not on his side.

A RIVALRY THAT TURNS INTO TEAMWORK

The duel establishes friction, but the story does not leave the pair in a loop of misunderstanding for the whole season. Ikki and Stella learn to respect one another, train, and share the emotional space the romance claims. That directness gives the relationship more forward motion than the premise initially suggests.

TOURNAMENT ACTION WITH PERSONAL STAKES

The academy’s duels provide a clean structure for showing how different Blazers fight. Ikki’s precision and restraint distinguish him from stronger opponents, while his resolve creates the season’s central underdog appeal. The 12-episode run moves quickly; some secondary characters and world details receive less attention than the central pair.

CONTENT AND EXPECTATIONS

Crunchyroll lists the series as not recommended for viewers under 16 and flags violence and sexualized imagery. Its opening includes nudity and a compromising situation. The romantic pairing is part of the appeal, but viewers expecting a fan-service-free school fantasy should look elsewhere.

WHO IS IT FOR?

A good fit for viewers who like magical academy duels, determined underdogs and romance that is allowed to become explicit rather than endlessly postponed. Less suitable if sexualized imagery or familiar tournament beats are a hard no.

VERDICT

Chivalry of a Failed Knight makes its underdog premise work by letting Ikki win through practice and judgment, while Stella develops into an active partner rather than a distant prize. The duel format and quick-moving romance are entertaining strengths; fan-service and the 16+ advisory are important caveats.
