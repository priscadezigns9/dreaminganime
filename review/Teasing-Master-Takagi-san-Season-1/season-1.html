A SMALL FORMAT WITH A CLEAR RHYTHM

This is a review of the complete first television season, Episodes 1–12. The season’s format gives its central relationships room to develop without pretending that every moment has equal weight. The gentle contest between Takagi and Nishikata is less about who wins a prank than about how they learn to read one another. Repeated teasing gives Nishikata fresh chances to plan a comeback, while small reactions reveal the fondness he cannot quite name. Its relaxed pace makes tiny shifts in confidence the payoff.

THE COMEBACK IS ONLY HALF THE GAME

Nishikata treats each exchange as a chance to regain the upper hand, but the pattern makes his reactions as important as the prank itself. Takagi’s teasing is a form of attention, and the recurring contests gradually show how much each notices about the other.

SMALL SHIFTS, EASY PACE

The season’s gentle rhythm suits a story built from repeated school-day encounters. A joke can reset the scene, yet familiarity gives the next attempt a slightly different meaning. Viewers seeking a high-stakes plot may find the repetition slight; the appeal is in watching awkwardness become a shared language.
