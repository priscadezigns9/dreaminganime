A SMALL FORMAT WITH A CLEAR RHYTHM

This is a review of the complete first television season, Episodes 1–13. The season’s format gives its central relationships room to develop without pretending that every moment has equal weight. Hori and Miyamura become a couple early, so the season’s interest shifts toward what intimacy changes. Their private selves complicate the roles classmates assign them, and knowing each other more closely alters how they see the wider group. The lightness of the school comedy works best when it makes room for those changing social perceptions.

AFTER THE COUPLE FORMS

Because Hori and Miyamura get together early, the relationship is a starting point rather than a final reward. The season can explore how they understand each other’s private selves and how that knowledge shifts the social picture around them.

INTIMACY CHANGES THE ROOM

The evolving couple is part of a broader school ensemble, so growing closer also affects how each character is perceived by others. That focus gives the show an easygoing, episodic feel, although the quick movement among supporting relationships can make some moments feel compressed.
