ISEKAI / SERIES REVIEW

I Got a Cheat Skill in Another World and Became Unrivaled in the Real World, Too

A dual-world power fantasy with an immediately appealing hook, brisk wish-fulfilment, and emotional stakes that can resolve too quickly.

QUICK TAKE

Yuuya’s movement between a magical world and everyday life gives this isekai power fantasy a clear hook and satisfying visual contrast. Its wish-fulfilment is easy to follow, but overwhelming power can dissolve conflict quickly, leaving emotional recovery and side characters underexplored.

THE DOOR THAT CHANGES THE SCALE

Yuuya has been bullied and neglected when he moves into his late grandfather’s home and discovers a door to a magical world. There, he gains powerful skills—and takes them back to Earth. That two-world premise is the series’ defining hook: fantasy rewards and real-world recognition are made parts of the same wish-fulfilment engine.

The contrast between a hostile everyday life and a realm where effort can quickly become power gives the opening an immediately legible emotional appeal. The fantasy setting is not only an escape; it changes how Yuuya is seen when he returns home. The show understands the satisfying fantasy of a hidden ability finally becoming visible.

A POWER FANTASY WITH A CLEAR VISUAL HOOK

The series’ main pleasure is the collision between the familiar and the fantastical. A discovery in one world can reframe what seems possible in the other, and that back-and-forth gives the premise its forward motion. Yuuya’s transformation and growing capability provide an easy-to-follow through-line for viewers who want an unabashedly aspirational isekai story.

That same design makes its conflicts difficult to sustain. Once Yuuya’s advantage becomes overwhelming, obstacles often feel temporary, and emotional consequences can be resolved before they have time to deepen. The story is more interested in the rush of a reversal than in the messy work of recovery or the long-term effects of being treated differently.

WHEN THE SUPPORTING CAST NEEDS MORE ROOM

The people around Yuuya help show how his changing circumstances affect his relationships, but many side characters have less interior space than the protagonist’s rapid ascent. Admiration and attraction can do too much of the work that distinct goals, disagreements, and independent growth might otherwise carry.

This is less a flaw for viewers seeking a straightforward power fantasy than a limit on the drama it can build. The series’ emotional starting point—bullying and neglect—suggests complicated wounds, while its wish-fulfilment rhythm tends to move past them quickly. More time with Yuuya’s inner life and the supporting cast would make the dual-world premise even richer.

A SEASON TWO UPDATE

Crunchyroll’s October 15, 2026 news report says Season 2 is slated for January 2027 and shares a new trailer and key visual. The announcement also introduces Iris and Mai. That is a release window, not a precise premiere date; viewers should check the official announcement for updates.

A second season has room to build on the original’s strongest idea: the same person moving between two worlds with very different rules. The opportunity is not simply to raise the power level, but to give the relationships and the emotional aftermath more space to keep pace with Yuuya’s abilities.

VERDICT

I Got a Cheat Skill in Another World and Became Unrivaled in the Real World, Too is an easy-to-read, high-gloss wish-fulfilment fantasy whose two-world structure gives its power fantasy an appealing twist. Its quick emotional resolutions, overpowered lead, and thinly developed supporting cast limit the drama, but viewers who want an uncomplicated transformation story may enjoy its brisk momentum. The announced second season offers a chance to deepen the characters as well as the spectacle.
