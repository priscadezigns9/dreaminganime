ANIME SERIES REVIEW · SEASON 1

Steins;Gate: Season 1

A scrappy lab’s experiments give a playful time-travel premise lasting consequences.

← ALL ANIME REVIEWS
Review scope: Steins;Gate original 2011 TV series, Season 1 · Episodes 1–24. This review excludes Steins;Gate 0, episode 23β, OVA/special episodes, and the film Load Region of Déjà Vu.
SPOILER-LIGHT · ORIGINAL TV SERIES · 24 EPISODES
QUICK TAKE

Steins;Gate gives its time-travel story a small, familiar starting point: a makeshift lab, a circle of friends, and experiments that seem more playful than consequential. The contrast is its strength. As the stakes rise, the ensemble and the rules of changing the past become inseparable.

THE LAB MAKES THE SCIENCE PERSONAL

The Future Gadget Laboratory is more than a place to introduce devices; it gives the early story a social center. Okabe’s performance as a self-styled mad scientist and the group’s banter make the speculative premise feel rooted in relationships before its implications widen.

CAUSE AND EFFECT TIGHTEN THE STORY

Time travel works here as a source of responsibility, not only a puzzle to solve. The possibility of changing the past creates a chain of choices that becomes harder to treat as harmless experimentation. The shift from playful curiosity to consequence gives the original series its main dramatic shape.

PATIENCE PAYS OFF

The 24-episode run allows the ensemble and its world to establish themselves before the central tension takes over. Viewers who enjoy character-led science fiction and a deliberate build should be well served; those looking for immediate high-speed plotting may need patience at the outset.

VERDICT

Steins;Gate is a character-centered time-travel story that turns the idea of changing the past into a question of responsibility. Its playful lab dynamic gives the later tension something human to threaten. This review covers the original 24-episode television series only and excludes Steins;Gate 0, special episodes, and the film.
