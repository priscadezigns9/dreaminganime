THE ELBAPH TENSION KEEPS RISING

The latest five available One Piece episodes create a strong run of escalating pressure as the Elbaph conflict moves from mystery into open danger. The verified schedule places episode 1170 on July 19, episode 1171 on July 26, episode 1174 on August 16, and episode 1175 after the review window, so this review focuses on the available 1170–1174 stretch rather than pretending the next episode has already aired. That distinction matters with a weekly series because anticipation can easily become confused with criticism. Episode 1174 gives the run its clearest immediate signal, with the official episode post describing the crisis as “Save the Children! The Elbaph Warriors Rise Up.” Dorry and Brogy return to the battlefield, while Scopper Gaban arrives with the Giant Warrior Pirates to help Elbaf fight. The story therefore widens the emotional field beyond the Straw Hats. The giants are not scenery. Their history, pride, and response to danger become part of the arc’s central movement. The strongest quality of this stretch is the way the setting begins to feel politically and emotionally unstable at the same time. Elbaph is beautiful, but beauty does not make it safe. The last five episodes work because every revelation increases the sense that the island’s future will be decided by more than one fight.

GIANTS, CHILDREN, AND THE COST OF WAR

What gives the current run its emotional weight is the contrast between legendary warriors and vulnerable children. The episode coverage points directly toward the danger facing the children of Elbaph, and that changes the meaning of the surrounding action. A battle is no longer only about reputation or territory. It is also about whether the strongest people in the setting will protect those who cannot protect themselves. That is a familiar One Piece theme, but the Elbaph setting gives it a particular scale. The giants carry a history of strength, yet the story keeps returning to the responsibility that comes with strength. Dorry and Brogy’s return is therefore more than a crowd-pleasing appearance. It is a statement about memory, loyalty, and the refusal to let a community be defined by fear. Scopper Gaban’s arrival expands that statement by bringing another legendary figure into the active conflict. The danger feels larger because the response is larger. At the same time, the arc does not lose its interest in individual reactions. Nami, Luffy, and the wider crew remain important because their choices translate the island’s crisis into the personal language of the Straw Hats. The episodes are most effective when spectacle and tenderness share the same frame. That is where One Piece continues to separate itself from a simple power contest.

THE VERDICT

The last five available episodes are a strong stretch of One Piece television, especially for viewers invested in the Elbaph arc’s history and giant-warrior mythology. The pacing is still designed for a long weekly adventure, so not every episode delivers a complete emotional conclusion. That is not a flaw by itself; the series is building a larger movement and using each episode to reposition the conflict. The important achievement is momentum. The mysterious monsters, the danger around the children, the return of Dorry and Brogy, and the arrival of Scopper Gaban all make the arc feel closer to a major turning point. The current run also demonstrates why One Piece benefits from its enormous cast and long memory. A name introduced earlier can return with emotional force because the world has kept moving in the background. My recommendation is to watch these five episodes together if possible, because the escalation is easier to feel as one continuous movement. Viewers who want immediate answers may find the weekly structure demanding, but viewers who enjoy layered world-building will find plenty to examine. The official Crunchyroll listing identifies the series as an action, adventure, comedy, fantasy, and shonen title, and this run uses all five qualities. It can be funny, strange, sentimental, and threatening without abandoning the central adventure. The verdict is simple: the Elbaph arc is gaining weight, and these five episodes are worth watching as a connected chapter rather than isolated weekly drops.
