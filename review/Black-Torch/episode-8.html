BLACK TORCH / EPISODE 8

“Underdog” turns two personal battles into a cliffhanger that changes Jiro and Rago’s partnership.

BROTHERS AND MONONOKE COLLIDE

“Underdog” puts Reiji’s confrontation with his brother Shinji alongside Rago’s reckoning with Amagi. The episode gives the brothers’ conflict more emotional context: a harsh household and years of comparison have turned old family wounds into a fight neither can treat as routine. Reiji’s composure now reads less like certainty and more like a way of carrying damage without showing it.

That personal struggle makes the action land harder. Shinji’s choices are not excused, but the episode lets the resentment and insecurity behind them come into view. The result is a more complicated rivalry than a simple clash between a good brother and a bad one, even as the fast-moving confrontation leaves some of the emotional fallout for later.

RAGO’S CHOICE CHANGES THE PARTNERSHIP

Rago and Amagi’s history gives their face-off a different weight. Amagi’s strength makes the threat feel immediate, but Rago refuses to surrender his judgment to fear. He leaves his spectral energy with Jiro, separating the power that has shaped their partnership from his own physical presence. The move is both an act of trust and a painful change in the bond at the centre of the series.

The ending keeps Rago’s fate deliberately uncertain. Amagi’s attack and Rago’s final words make the moment feel like a possible loss, while the energy left with Jiro means their connection is not simply erased. That ambiguity is an effective cliffhanger because it gives Jiro a new responsibility without pretending the audience has all the answers.

A TURNING POINT WITH A COST

Episode 8 is among the series’ more emotionally concentrated chapters. It gives Reiji and Shinji’s history room to matter, lets Rago make a consequential choice, and leaves Jiro facing the consequences of power he did not earn alone. The pace is brisk enough that some grief and motivation could use more breathing room, but the episode’s central decisions give the story a sharper direction.

VERDICT

“Underdog” works as a turning point: Reiji’s family conflict becomes personal, Rago stands firm against Amagi, and Jiro is left carrying a new kind of responsibility. Its unanswered ending is a strength rather than a flaw, as long as the next episode follows through on the emotional cost of Rago’s choice.
