BLACK TORCH / EPISODE 5

Koga’s confrontation tests Jiro and Rago’s bond—and the series’ promise of a bigger fight.

Episode 5 spoilers ahead. This review discusses the Koga confrontation and character revelations.
QUICK TAKE

Episode 5 centers on Koga’s confrontation: the action under-delivers on escalation while Jiro, Rago, and Ichika’s character stories deepen.

THE FIGHT AND WHAT IT REVEALS

Episode 5 puts Koga’s confrontation at the center of the story. The cave setting keeps the action easy to follow, but the clash relies on short bursts, impact effects, and pauses instead of building sustained danger. With so much anticipation around the fight, that stop-start rhythm leaves the showdown feeling smaller than its setup.

The more lasting change comes from how Jiro and Rago respond to Koga’s attempt to define them by their labels. Their connection becomes a choice they make together, not just a source of extra power. Reiji also has to reckon with the limits of his own strength, while Jiro begins to question what he knows about his father. The conflict moves their relationships forward even when the fight itself does not fully escalate.

ICHIKA’S STORY ADDS WEIGHT

Ichika’s memories give the second half its emotional center. The shift from warm childhood scenes to the colder images surrounding her mother reframes her grief as a concrete goal. That personal thread lands more clearly than the central battle because the episode gives it a visual and emotional progression, not just another piece of lore.

TOO MANY THREADS AT ONCE

The episode opens several doors quickly: Jiro’s father, Reiji’s brother, Ichika’s mother, and a new threat at the end. Each could carry real weight, but introduced together they compete for room. The character beats make the episode worthwhile; the action still needed more momentum to feel like the promised climax.

VERDICT

Episode 5 works better as a character chapter than as an action showcase. The Koga fight under-delivers on escalation, but Jiro and Rago’s bond deepens, Ichika gains a clearer purpose, and the team begins to feel like a choice rather than an assignment. The next episode has several promising threads to pick up; it will be strongest if it gives one or two of them space to breathe.
