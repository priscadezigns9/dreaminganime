VIVY -FLUORITE EYE'S SONG- / REVIEW

A CENTURY IN EVERY SONG

← ALL ANIME REVIEWS
Review scope: Vivy -Fluorite Eye's Song- original TV anime, Season 1 · Episodes 1–13. This review covers the complete 13-episode series and excludes unrelated media.
Crunchyroll content advisory: Not recommended for minors under sixteen; Violence, Blood/Gore. Regional availability and rating labels can vary.

A MISSION MEASURED IN YEARS

Vivy is an autonomous android performer whose daily goal is to make people happy through song. When Matsumoto arrives with a warning about a future conflict, the two begin a long intervention across a century of turning points. The official series listing frames the story around a singer, a time-travelling AI, and an effort to alter what lies ahead.

MUSIC, MEMORY, AND CHANGE

The 13-episode premise centers on a century-long attempt to change a future war, setting up tension between a fixed mission and a changing world. Singing is integral to Vivy's stated purpose, so the AI conflict is paired with questions of identity and what it means to make people happy. The time-spanning structure also makes the consequences of each intervention part of the show's core idea.

FINAL VERDICT

Vivy's central idea pairs an AI science-fiction story with the question of whether a directive can become a chosen purpose. The official series page describes a 100-year journey; Netflix and the official U.S. Blu-ray page list 13 episodes. Crunchyroll flags violence and blood/gore, a useful counterpoint to the music-forward premise.
