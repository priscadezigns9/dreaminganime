NARUTO / SERIES REVIEW

NARUTO

Team 7, the long road to Hokage, and a story about choosing connection over hatred.

← BACK TO REVIEWS
FRANCHISE REVIEW · ORIGINAL NARUTO + SHIPPUDEN
QUICK TAKE

Naruto’s original series and Shippuden build a sprawling ninja saga around a simple emotional need: an ignored child wants to be seen, then learns that belonging is something he can create for others too. Team 7 and the series’ best character arcs make the long journey memorable. Filler, repeated flashbacks, and uneven pacing are the main costs of entry.

MORE THAN A DREAM OF BECOMING HOKAGE

Naruto begins as the child the Hidden Leaf Village would rather ignore. He wants to become Hokage, but beneath the loud pranks and impossible confidence is a more basic wish: to be acknowledged and to have a place among people who choose him. That emotional centre gives the story more staying power than its early rivalry-and-training setup might suggest.

The original series introduces Team 7—Naruto, Sasuke, Sakura, and Kakashi—and builds its strongest early stretch around missions that test both skill and trust. Naruto’s stubborn hope is not simply a joke or a shortcut to victory; it becomes a challenge to characters who have learned to expect betrayal, loss, or isolation.

SHIPPUDEN WIDENS THE CONFLICT

Naruto: Shippuden carries that foundation into a larger, more consequential story. The threat of the Akatsuki, Sasuke’s path away from the village, and the wider history of the shinobi world push the series toward questions about revenge, inherited conflict, and whether understanding can interrupt violence.

The saga is strongest when it lets a character’s history change how we read their choices. Even its antagonists are often given understandable wounds, though explanation does not always excuse their actions. That tension—between compassion and accountability—is one of the story’s most compelling ideas.

WHAT STILL WORKS

Team 7 remains the emotional anchor. Naruto and Sasuke’s bond is messy, intense, and central to the series’ argument about connection; Sakura and Kakashi add their own histories and forms of loyalty. The wider cast gives the ninja world texture, and the best fights communicate character through strategy, sacrifice, and restraint rather than spectacle alone.

For all its scale, Naruto keeps returning to a human question: what happens when people who have been hurt decide whether to pass that hurt on? The answer is sometimes idealistic, but the story earns its optimism by showing how difficult it is to reach someone who has stopped expecting care.

THE LONG-RUN COST

This is a major time commitment, and not every stretch earns its length. Filler arcs can interrupt momentum, flashbacks and repeated explanations sometimes slow scenes that already made their point, and some supporting characters receive less development than their introductions promise. The later power escalation also risks making quieter ninja tactics feel less important.

Those are real drawbacks, not reasons to dismiss the series. Viewers who want a tighter route can use a reputable episode guide to separate the core story from anime-original material; viewers who enjoy spending time with a large cast may find the detours part of the experience.

VERDICT

Naruto and Shippuden are still defining long-form shonen: emotionally direct, built around memorable rivalries, and strongest when action grows from character. The series can be uneven and padded, but its themes of belonging, grief, and refusing to inherit hatred give the journey a lasting centre. Start with the original Naruto, then continue into Shippuden if the characters and world have you invested.

QUICK ANSWERS

What does this Naruto review cover?

This review considers the original Naruto anime and its direct continuation, Naruto: Shippuden. It does not cover Boruto or the new Naruto × Ninku limited series.

Should I watch Naruto before Naruto: Shippuden?

Yes. Shippuden continues the characters and story established in the original series, so starting with Naruto gives the later relationships and conflicts their context.

Is Naruto worth watching?

It is worth watching for its character bonds, emotional backstories, and long-form shonen payoff. Filler and uneven pacing can make a curated viewing plan appealing.

What new Naruto projects were announced in 2026?

A new Masashi Kishimoto manga titled Naruto Shadow Rising and a four-episode Naruto × Ninku anime limited series were announced. The anime has no announced release date yet.
