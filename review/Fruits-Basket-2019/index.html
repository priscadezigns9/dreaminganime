Fruits Basket (2019)

A gentle new home opens the door to a family secret, an ensemble story, and the hard work of healing.

← BACK TO REVIEWS
SPOILER-LIGHT · FULL TV ADAPTATION REVIEW · 2019 REBOOT · SEASONS 1–3
QUICK TAKE

This review is specifically about the 2019 Fruits Basket reboot: its three-season television story, not the 2001 adaptation and not the separate film Fruits Basket: Prelude. It begins with Tohru Honda’s practical kindness and a strange Zodiac curse, then gradually reveals that the Sohma family’s supernatural secret is also a story about fear, isolation, and the ways people learn to accept care.

A NEW HOME WITH A STRANGE RULE

After losing her mother, Tohru tries to manage life on her own. A chance discovery brings her into the home of Yuki, Kyo, and Shigure Sohma, where she learns that members of the family transform into Zodiac animals when embraced by someone of the opposite sex. The premise gives the opening a bright, almost fairy-tale surprise, but it quickly becomes the doorway into a much larger family story.

TOHRU’S KINDNESS IS AN ACTIVE CHOICE

Tohru’s warmth can look impossibly effortless, yet the series gives it weight by showing how much she has had to endure and how carefully she pays attention to others. She is not simply a cure for the Sohmas’ pain. Her patience creates room for people to speak honestly, while the show keeps asking what compassion can and cannot change when a family has built its habits around a curse.

AN ENSEMBLE THAT DEEPENS OVER TIME

The early episodes introduce a lively housemate comedy, with Tohru, Yuki, Kyo, and Shigure forming a memorable core. As more of the Sohma family enter the story, the series shifts into a broader ensemble drama. Different members have very different relationships to the curse and to the family’s expectations; that variety makes the supernatural hook feel personal rather than decorative.

FROM COMFORT TO CONSEQUENCE

The reboot’s long form is one of its greatest strengths. Lighter scenes and affectionate comedy make its world inviting, while later developments give the characters’ emotional patterns real consequences. The tonal shifts can be intense, but they are part of a story that wants kindness to matter without pretending that kindness alone erases harm. Across its three TV seasons, the characters are allowed time to change.

WHY THE 2019 VERSION STANDS APART

The 2001 adaptation is a separate earlier version of the manga, not the scope of this review. Crunchyroll’s contemporary preview described the 2019 series as a new take intended to reach the end of the story; this page considers the finished 2019 television adaptation as a whole. Fruits Basket: Prelude is a separate film and is not included here.

VERDICT

Fruits Basket (2019) earns its emotional reach through patience. It starts with an inviting supernatural-comedy premise, then develops into a layered story about family, grief, shame, and the difficult practice of letting people close. The ensemble gives the series range, and Tohru’s kindness provides a steady center without making the other characters’ healing feel automatic. For viewers open to a slow-building, often tender drama with heavier turns, this is a complete and rewarding reboot.
