A SMALL FORMAT WITH A CLEAR RHYTHM

This is a review of the complete first television season, Episodes 1–12. The season’s format gives its central relationships room to develop without pretending that every moment has equal weight. The season is interested in what happens after rejection, when the presumed lead has to keep living among the people and routines left behind. Its unexpected circle gives those characters space to recover without turning heartbreak into a simple setup for the next romance. The comedy has a tender undercurrent whenever it lets disappointment be real before moving forward.

WHAT COMES AFTER REJECTION

The premise starts from people who have not won the romance they hoped for, then stays with the aftermath instead of treating it as a brief setup. That shift lets disappointment and self-consciousness belong to the characters, not merely serve as a gag.

A CIRCLE THAT IS NOT A REPLACEMENT

The unexpected group gives the heroines company, but companionship is not presented as an instant substitute for what they lost. The comedy works best when it leaves the hurt intact long enough to matter. That choice gives the season a more generous view of recovery than a simple “next romance” formula.
